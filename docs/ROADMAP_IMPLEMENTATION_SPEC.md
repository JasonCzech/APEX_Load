1. Extend the existing owners: pure measurement semantics in `pkg/analysis` and `pkg/rca`; protocol capture in `pkg/protocol`; durable intent/CAS in `pkg/storage`; orchestration/publication in `pkg/api` and `pkg/distributed`; shared dashboard authoring and report models. 2. Old archives stay readable. Absent, unsupported, censored, stale, reset, truncated and resource-limited evidence never becomes a measured zero or a healthy verdict.
3. Use monotonic time inside a process and qualified mappings across clocks. Preserve uncertainty and invalidation on clock steps. Merge duration sketches or compatible legacy distributions, never percentiles. An uncertain threshold crossing is unscored; a positive lower bound must remain visible without becoming an exact total.
4. Additive capabilities must be negotiated at validation, placement and staging. An explicitly requested unsupported combination fails with a typed reason before target traffic. New JSON/DSL/API fields belong to the existing catalog/schema owners; regenerate generated artifacts only when those contracts change.
5. Durable publication checks project/input deletion, object incarnation, revision and ownership generation after artifacts are durable. Ambiguous outcomes retain retry evidence; cancellation revokes publication. Late writers enter the existing cleanup path. Schema changes include SQLite/PostgreSQL migrations, indexes, retention, downgrade/read behavior and restart tests in the same package.
6. Keep current authorization, secret/egress boundaries and viewer/editor/admin distinctions. Recheck authorization at read, export and publication. Reports, HAR and diagnostic samples use the shared redaction boundaries. Analytical and evidence requests cannot select arbitrary SQL, filesystem paths, code or infrastructure cleanup; existing governed execution protocols retain their own contracts.
7. Every bounded queue, result set, artifact and job names its population/limit and exposes rejected or truncated work. Reuse current quotas; any changed default requires measured resource evidence and migration notes. Feature flags may stop new work without making existing evidence unreadable.

### REC-A — Recover every evacuation interruption boundary

Extend the durable evacuation journal through conceptual phases `prepared → staged → source_fenced → replacement_committed → completed`, mapped onto existing states rather than creating a second coordinator. Persist exact source/replacement identities, execution/owner generations, capacity and topology observations, script/capability pins, pause state and remaining active duration. Make each side effect idempotent under conditional ownership checks.

Current successor adoption covers a narrow committed-replacement case. Add earlier interruption phases and a missing replacement for the eligible HTTP workload. Prepared/staged/source-fenced recovery remains fail-closed until its new replay/fence path is qualified (except the opt-in rollback, roll-forward and replacement restart below); old peers cannot silently enter the new recovery states. A successor first proves what was committed and which execution can still run; it cannot infer a safe restart from an absent heartbeat. Never run old and replacement generations concurrently, replay already completed initialization, resurrect a stopped run or extend its configured active duration. Missing safe capacity remains an explicit admission/recovery state.

The [evacuation rollback slice](qa/reviews/roadmap-evacuation-rollback-2026-10-22/README.md) adds a version 4 journal, behind `APEXLOAD_EVACUATION_ROLLBACK_RECOVERY`, that records the replacement capacity claims and the standby pre-images. A successor returns a prepared or staged evacuation of a running or paused run to its prior topology. It adopts the prior shards and fences each replacement with an epoch claim that carries no adoption for the run. It then releases the replacement's capacity, reserving an activated standby idle again afterwards, and only then replaces the journal. A rollback that cannot be completed keeps the journal and holds the run `STOPPING`. A leader that rolls its own staging back restores the prior checkpoint once the ledger shows the capacity returned. Until then it marks the journal abandoned, as does a later re-arm that finds a journal its leader left behind. A successor never rolls back to an abandoned journal. Source-fenced journals, abandoned journals, version 3 journals and every journal with the setting off remain fail-closed, and older peers cannot read version 4. A missing-replacement restart for the eligible HTTP workload, replay of source-fenced work and the failure-injection exit below remain open.

The [evacuation roll-forward slice](qa/reviews/roadmap-evacuation-rollforward-2026-10-24/README.md) adds a version 5 journal, behind `APEXLOAD_EVACUATION_ROLLFORWARD_RECOVERY` together with the rollback setting. It records the replacement's staged duration and pause state, and a `replacement_committing` mark with the exact duration shortening, written before `CommitWork` is sent. A successor finishes such an evacuation of a running or paused run: it records a `rolling_forward` intent, adopts the survivors and the replacement in one epoch claim, applies the recorded duration with the rotated token, commits and transfers the replacement capacity, and only then replaces the journal. Only an adopted replacement proves its commit: one that never committed is refused and drained by that claim, and one reporting a completed run is refused too, because a stopped staged assignment reports the same. The run is then held `STOPPING`, as is any roll-forward that cannot be completed; the hold is written before any adopted assignment is force-stopped, for rollback as well. A version 5 journal held at `source_fenced` now states that its replacement never committed. Version 3 and 4 `source_fenced` journals, abandoned journals, `STOPPING` runs and every journal with either setting off remain fail-closed, and older peers cannot read version 5. Deployed failure injection and independent target counters remain open. A participant that reports a completed run the prior lineage never recorded also holds the run, for rollback as well, because a controller stop ends the same way on the generator, unless its generator reports the run's duration ran out: the [generator terminal cause slice](qa/reviews/roadmap-generator-terminal-cause-2026-10-24/README.md) adds an additive `RunAdoptionResult` terminal cause and logical duration, retained across generator restarts in the archive manifest, and both recoveries trust a `DURATION_ELAPSED` completion at no less than the journaled commit target (replacement) or the run's durable duration (any other participant). A naturally completed replacement rolls forward without a duration adjustment, its pending reservation is released, and natural completions are recorded in the replacing checkpoint. Older generators, archives and pruned tombstones report no cause and still hold the run. Version 3 and 4 `source_fenced` journals lack the version 5 fields and stay permanently fail-closed until fenced or attested. The run's own deadline elapsing before the successor is exercised only with fake generators (the `staged`, `rolling_back`, `replacement_committing` and `rolling_forward` phases) and an in-process replacement; the `prepared` phase and an in-process survivor or source whose duration elapsed are not. At this phase the exit's capability mismatch, missing spare capacity, a stop during roll-forward, worker/node partition, rolling-version refusal by older controllers during an upgrade, and pause at the `rolling_forward` phase across a second leader change were not yet exercised; the completion slice below exercises all of them but the partition, the rolling-version refusal only as a simulation.

The [evacuation replacement restart slice](qa/reviews/roadmap-evacuation-replacement-restart-2026-10-24/README.md) closes the missing-replacement case behind `APEXLOAD_EVACUATION_REPLACEMENT_RESTART`, together with both settings above. When the source was fenced and the replacement never committed (a version 5 or 6 `source_fenced` journal), or a roll-forward finds its replacement not adopted, a successor of a running or paused run restarts the moved share on a new replacement. It first proves that nothing runs it: the fenced source is never claimed, and the old replacement's acknowledged epoch claim must answer with no live assignment (`REJECTED`, `UNKNOWN_TEST`, a completion, or a terminal stop); an unreachable or still-running old replacement holds the run as before. Only a successor writes version 6 journals: a `replacement_restarting` intent before any generator sees the rotated token, and then, through the ordinary generator-loss reshard in Phase B, the restart's own evacuation journal, whose source is recorded absent rather than stopped. The share's journaled reservation becomes the run's under the new term and seeds the new placement: one generator takes every moved group, and never the old replacement, the fenced source or an archive target, which the journal records. The share runs at most to its journaled logical end and the run's durable remaining duration, pauses included, starts paused with a paused run, and keeps its shard identity with fresh VUsers, as the reshard does. Each journal boundary across a further leader change is resumable (the intent, the restart's journal before `replacement_committing`) or finishes as a roll-forward or promotion, and a restart's `replacement_committing` journal whose new replacement was not adopted restarts again; a late old owner is refused by the lease and the generators. Refusals hold the run `STOPPING` with a reason: the setting off, an ineligible workload, an unprovable duration, no spare capacity, an unreachable or still-running old replacement. The failover record and report notices say the share was restarted. Older peers cannot read version 6, and with the setting off every version 6 journal but a committed one is held. A restart onto the same generator, evacuations with more than one replacement, a replacement whose generator process restarted when pod reservations are enabled, loss-tolerant runs with a survivor, live-table runs, and deployed qualification remain open.

The [evacuation completion slice](qa/reviews/roadmap-evacuation-completion-2026-10-25/README.md) closes three local gaps, with no new setting and no journal change.
